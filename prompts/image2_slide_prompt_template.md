# Image2 slide mockup prompt template

Generate **one finished presentation slide image**, not a photo of a screen, a slide mockup on a desk, a collage, or a multi-slide contact sheet.

## Canvas and output
- One slide only, 16:9 landscape, 1920 × 1080 pixels.
- Use the entire canvas edge-to-edge. No device frame, app chrome, watermark, border, or external scene.
- The output is a visual target for later reconstruction as native editable PowerPoint objects.

## Content fidelity
- Treat the supplied slide claim, exact on-slide strings, source references, and scientific constraints as locked content.
- Reproduce every required on-slide string verbatim, preserving language, symbols, capitalization, numbers, units, subscripts, superscripts, and punctuation.
- Do not invent measurements, results, citations, labels, arrows, causal relations, or technical details.
- Do not move speaker notes onto the slide. Speaker notes are context for the design only.
- If a source does not establish a detail, omit it or retain the supplied schematic/evidence-boundary wording.

## Visual composition
- Establish a clear title, one dominant visual message, and a deliberate reading path.
- Use a restrained, coherent visual system with clear contrast and projection-scale typography.
- Prefer a clean grid, aligned regions, consistent spacing, and generous whitespace.
- Make the main scientific diagram visually legible: distinguish nodes, modules, connectors, arrowheads, labels, and evidence boundaries.
- Use visual hierarchy rather than adding decorative boxes around every sentence.
- Keep labels large enough to read at presentation distance. If the content does not fit, simplify the composition or split the slide; do not shrink text to micro-size.

## PowerPoint reconstruction constraints
- Prefer simple, separable geometric elements: text regions, rectangles, circles, lines, arrows, chart marks, and small icons.
- Avoid full-canvas photographic backgrounds, dense textures, complex 3D effects, tiny ornamental lines, and decorative effects that cannot be reproduced as native objects.
- Do not rasterize text into a decorative texture. Keep text visually distinct from illustration.
- Use photographs or complex scientific imagery only when they are part of the supplied source material and must remain images.
- Keep the design visually polished but practical to rebuild from editable text boxes, shapes, connectors, and charts.

## Required review
The image is a **candidate**, not an approved scientific figure. It will be checked for exact text, scientific correctness, source fidelity, visual hierarchy, projection readability, and reconstruction feasibility before any PPTX work begins.
